The EPIC Division is a network of teams for children of all ages with physical and developmental challenges of any sort who want to play soccer but require extra help to do so. The EPIC Division is coached by parents and other volunteers.

EPIC seeks both team members and "buddies." Buddies are more experienced soccer players (generally ten years old and up) who work with the EPIC players as peer coaches and helpers. If you are interested in becoming a EPIC Buddy, please contact [Serhat Krause](mailto:epic@wssl.org) or [Joshua Noble](mailto:joshuafnoble@gmail.com) of the EPIC Program for more information.

The EPIC Division plays ONLY during the Fall season.

EPIC & Mainstream Practice Partners

Each weekend, a Mainstream team will practice and play with each of our EPIC teams. Below are additional details about the structure of the session and the expectations for the MPPs. But first, the philosophy-- the "why".

Why are we doing this?

During the 2012 and 2013 seasons we ran "pilot programs," and saw the benefits to the EPIC teams of having mainstream opponents -- it speeds their development as players, and calls forth extra effort from them. And we have been amazed at the benefits it brings to the mainstream opponents -- it visibly fosters their development as empathetic citizens of the world. It gives pre-teens and teenagers an opportunity to step outside of themselves and interact with someone who needs their help. Parents get a chance to see the living example of their child's best potential. Together we build a more tolerant and diverse society. The experience has been a magical one for EPIC and mainstream alike.

And, it turns out that New York City was 20 years behind the times. At the National Games in July 2014, we met Peter James Gaughan, who helps run EPIC in Concord, CA. They are about the same scale as us-- 65 players vs the 90+ in WSSL. And they have been playing EPIC vs mainstream teams since 1994. It has been a wonderful thing for both teams, and has long-ago become part of the DNA of their region. Peter reports that the magic remains, even after 20 years of routine EPIC vs Mainstream play.

In 2014 NYC caught up with California.

Schedule/Format of the Sessions

The session starts with a group practice, during which one or more mainstream players assists each EPIC player. During the actual game, for the youngest groups the mainstream players continue to function as "practice buddies." For the oldest, the mainstream players are half-speed opponents, adjusting their skill sets to match their EPIC opponents. We are not looking for the games to be a “giveaway” to the EPIC team. Instead looking to present them with a challenge that must be overcome. Or not overcome, if they don't put in the effort. That is part of what happens in sports.

Play on the Field

For the actual games in Central Park, we aim to provide a motivating level of opposition to the EPIC team. For our youngest players, we just let them score and celebrate that.

But as our players age, they need nuanced opposition. For our U-10 and above EPIC teams, in the ideal game the EPIC team would be on the losing side of a down-by-one low-scoring game for most of the match. And EPIC would only even-up the score when they upped their energy level and their passing game.

For each game, the mainstream coaches and players, will use two simple hand signals. One is thrusting forward the arms, palms out, meaning "push more." The other is the arms overhead, left hand clasping the right wrist: "hold back." The experience in Concord has been that the mainstream players in general need to be urged to push more, to give more opposition. Who would imagine: Teenagers are usually too nice. The EPIC coach will help guide the mainstream team, working through their coach.

**Rising Stars. 60 minute session.** EPIC players ages 5-8, with 0-3 yrs in league. MPPs G12. Emphasis on skill building, with some play each week by highly modified rules. Games are run “short-sided” with as few as three players per side.

**Younger Minors. 90 minute session.** EPIC ages 9-11. Experienced players, generally with 2+ years in league. MPPs B12. Wide, wide range of skills and readiness. Continued skill building, but emphasis on play compared to Rising Stars. Still play short-sided, usually three or four per side.

**Older Minors. 90 minute session.** EPIC ages 11-14. MPPs G13. Similar to Younger Minors.

**Majors. 90 minute session.** EPIC ages 13-19. MPPs G14 and G19. Continued skill building, and about half the session is spent in game time. About five or six players have very high support needs, and need one or two on-field buddies. The rest are typically able to play a refereed game, with coaches off the field.

**Majors+. 90 minute session.** EPIC ages 19 and older. MPPs B19. Similar to Majors.
